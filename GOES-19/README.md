
# GOES-19 Satellite Imagery Viewer

## Purpose

<p align="justify">
This viewer presents NOAA GOES-19 satellite imagery for monitoring atmospheric conditions, cloud development, severe weather, and environmental phenomena. The imagery products support meteorological analysis and visualization through animated satellite observations.
</p>

<br>
<br>

## Animated Imagery Products

### Composite Imagery

<ul>
<li><p align="justify">GeoColor: Used frequently by meteorologists for monitoring cloud cover, weather-system movement, and daytime and nighttime weather patterns.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify">GLM FED3 + GeoColor: Used for identifying lightning activity, thunderstorm development, and convective intensity.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify">AirMass RGB: Used for identifying air masses, jet streams, upper-level disturbances, and stratospheric air intrusions.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify">Sandwich RGB: Used for examining severe thunderstorms, overshooting cloud tops, and convective storm structure.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify">Day Night Cloud Micro Combo RGB: Used for distinguishing cloud types, cloud-top phase, low clouds, and nighttime fog.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify">Fire Temperature: Used for detecting active wildfires, thermal hotspots, and changes in fire activity.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify">Dust RGB: Used for identifying airborne dust, tracking dust plumes, and monitoring atmospheric dust transport.</p></li>
</ul>

<br>
<br>

### Spectral Bands

<ul>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 1:</strong> Blue Visible (0.47 µm): Used for detecting atmospheric aerosols, smoke, haze, and dust during daylight.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 2:</strong> Red Visible (0.64 µm): Used for monitoring cloud development, thunderstorm initiation, convective boundaries, and fine-scale cloud structures.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 3:</strong> Vegetation / Near-Infrared (0.86 µm): Used for distinguishing vegetation, land and water boundaries, flooding impacts, and surface changes.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 4:</strong> Cirrus / Near-Infrared (1.37 µm): Used for detecting thin, high-altitude cirrus clouds that may be difficult to distinguish in conventional visible imagery.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 5:</strong> Snow/Ice / Near-Infrared (1.6 µm): Used for distinguishing snow and ice from clouds and identifying cloud-phase differences during daylight.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 6:</strong> Cloud Particle / Near-Infrared (2.2 µm): Used for analyzing cloud particle size, cloud phase, snow characteristics, and very hot surface features.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 7:</strong> Shortwave Window / Infrared (3.9 µm): Used for detecting wildfire hotspots, nighttime fog, low clouds, and temperature contrasts.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 8:</strong> Upper-Level Water Vapor / Infrared (6.2 µm): Used for tracking upper-tropospheric moisture, jet-stream disturbances, and atmospheric waves.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 9:</strong> Mid-Level Water Vapor / Infrared (6.9 µm): Used for examining mid-tropospheric moisture, atmospheric circulation, and developing weather disturbances.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 10:</strong> Lower-Level Water Vapor / Infrared (7.3 µm): Used for identifying lower-tropospheric moisture patterns, dry-air intrusions, and atmospheric disturbances.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 11:</strong> Cloud-Top Phase / Infrared (8.4 µm): Used for distinguishing cloud-top phase and investigating volcanic ash and cloud microphysical characteristics.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 12:</strong> Ozone / Infrared (9.6 µm): Used for examining upper-atmospheric ozone patterns and identifying tropopause-related disturbances.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 13:</strong> Clean Longwave Window / Infrared (10.3 µm): Used for monitoring cloud-top temperatures, thunderstorm development, tropical cyclones, and atmospheric motion.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 14:</strong> Longwave Window / Infrared (11.2 µm): Used for monitoring cloud-top temperatures, storm intensity, and surface temperature patterns.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 15:</strong> Dirty Longwave Window / Infrared (12.3 µm): Used for analyzing low-level atmospheric moisture, cloud properties, and temperature differences between infrared channels.</p></li>
<li><p align="justify"><strong>Band 16:</strong> Carbon Dioxide / Infrared (13.3 µm): Used for estimating cloud-top heights, identifying atmospheric temperature structures, and supporting cloud analysis.</p></li>
</ul>

## Animation Standard

<p align="justify">
Animation length: 72 frames, Imagery interval: 5 minutes, Animation window: 6 hours, First-to-last frame span: 5 hours and 55 minutes, Imagery updates: subject to NOAA product availability (usually every 5 minutes).
</p>
